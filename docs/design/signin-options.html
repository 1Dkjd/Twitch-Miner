Two ways to sign in to Twitch

Both are tested on your account. Below: what each one costs you, then every screen you'd see, at real size, in the approved Mining look.

Option A

Code sign-in + campaign catalogue

You get an 8-letter code, enter it at twitch.tv/activate on any device, done.

Pros

  • Sign in from anywhere. Any phone or computer, in your own browser, so your password manager and 2FA work as usual.
  • The login doesn't expire. Twitch reported no end date in the test. Twitch can still end it.
  • No browser inside Docker. About 400 MB smaller and fewer moving parts.
  • Earns minutes 1:1. Tested: 12 minutes watched, +12 on your Tarkov drop.

Cons

  • New campaigns come from a third party. An anonymous public feed (Sunkwi). If it stops, campaigns you've started keep mining, but new ones go unnoticed. Priority games get a partial backup lookup.
  • Account links are unknown until a campaign starts. Twitch hides them from this login. Proposed fix: if a campaign's minutes don't move for about 10 minutes, it moves to Needs you as "may need linking".
  • Claiming not yet proven here. Another miner reports it works. The first real claim confirms it.
  • Twitch already hid the campaign list from this login. It could restrict it further.
Option B

Browser sign-in inside the page

Twitch's real login page runs in the container and shows live inside Drops Miner.

Pros

  • Everything comes from Twitch. Full campaign list (152 for you today) and account links, with no third party.
  • Proven end to end. It's what is mining for you now, claims included.
  • Same approach as your dungfu container.

Cons

  • Typing into a streamed browser. Password managers can't fill it, and phones need a keyboard button. The paste box and keyboard button are unproven fixes.
  • Bigger, fiddlier container. Chromium, a virtual screen and VNC: about 400 MB, plus phase 5 setup work.
  • Relies on rewriting Twitch's login request. That trick breaks if Twitch changes its login page. It changed sign-in once already, in September.
  • How long the login lasts is unmeasured. The current one has held since 9 October.

My take: A is the nicer product, and the test says it mines. Its real weak spots are the third-party feed and unknown account links. B is the safer engine, with clunkier sign-in and a heavier container. Either way, the other one can stay in the code as a manual fallback.
